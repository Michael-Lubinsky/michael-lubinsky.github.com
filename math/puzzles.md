##
The areas of triangles ADE, ECF and AFB are known.
What is area of triangle AEF?
<img width="487" height="339" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3373c065-6460-4d30-bb00-2fffec4fa9c3" />

Let the three known areas be

$$ S\_{ADE}=P,\qquad S\_{ECF}=Q,\qquad S\_{AFB}=R. $$

We want to find \\(S\_{AEF}=X\\).

We want to find $S\_{AEF}=X$

The key observation is that the area of the rectangle is determined by these three areas, even though its side lengths are unknown.

Let the rectangle have width $w$ and height $h$. Define

$$ t=\frac{DE}{DC},\qquad u=\frac{BF}{BC}. $$

Then

$$ P=\frac{wh}{2}t,\qquad R=\frac{wh}{2}u, $$

and

$$ Q=\frac{wh}{2}(1-t)(1-u). $$

Set \\(K=wh/2\\). Since \\(t=P/K\\) and \\(u=R/K\\),

$$ Q=K\left(1-\frac PK\right) \left(1-\frac RK\right). $$

Expanding,

$$ Q=K-P-R+\frac{PR}{K}. $$

Thus

$$ K^2-(P+Q+R)K+PR=0. $$

Because $K\geq P$ and $K\geq R$, the geometrically valid root is

$$ K=\frac{P+Q+R+\sqrt{(P+Q+R)^2-4PR}}{2}. $$

The rectangle has area $2K$, and the three outer triangles have total area $P+Q+R$. Therefore

$$ X=2K-(P+Q+R). $$

Substituting gives the remarkably simple result:

$$ \boxed{\displaystyle S\_{AEF}=\sqrt{(P+Q+R)^2-4PR} } $$

Or, in terms of the original triangles,

$$ \boxed{ S\_{AEF}= \sqrt{ (S\_{ADE}+S\_{ECF}+S\_{AFB})^2 -4S\_{ADE}S\_{AFB} } } $$
